## 2. The 5 AI Questions (Judge Q&A Cheat Sheet)

Be prepared to answer these exact questions from the jury, based on the engineering truths of the system:

**1. What model did you use and why?**
> We use an **LightGBM model** over geographical districts. LightGBM excels at handling tabular, mixed-type features (like ours), effectively manages class imbalances, provides calibrated probabilities for our confidence thresholds, and offers gain-based explanations for feature importance. 

**2. What data was it trained on?**
> The model is trained on a synthetic dataset of 10,000 complaints and transaction chains. This data was generated by a documented behavioral simulation that is strictly calibrated to **publicly reported cyber-fraud hotspot patterns** and **RBI/I4C aggregate statistics**. By design, it contains zero real PII, but the architecture is ready to be refined with real I4C data under NDA.

**3. What is the accuracy and test set?**
> We report Top-1, Top-3, and Top-5 district accuracy on a **temporally held-out test set**. Crucially, we measure this against the **majority-class** (always guess the biggest hotspot) and **nearest-district** baselines. Our model beats both by a clear margin, proving it has generalized real routing behavior. Our confidence is also calibrated (e.g., measured via Brier score).

**4. What happens when the AI is wrong?**
> We implemented a strict confidence threshold (τ). If the prediction's confidence falls below this threshold, **it is not auto-dispatched**. Instead, it is routed to an Analyst Review queue for human-in-the-loop validation. Furthermore, all outcomes are logged to continuously measure and improve the real-world interception rate.

**5. Why use AI over simple rules (like distance)?**
> Because rules fail against sophisticated syndicates. A nearest-district rule completely misses the fact that mule networks purposefully route funds cross-state to specific hotspots to evade local police. Our model learns these non-obvious, asymmetric routing behaviors—and we mathematically prove this by showing our Top-3 accuracy significantly outperforms the distance-based baseline.
